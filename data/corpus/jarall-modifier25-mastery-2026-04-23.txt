The JARALL Standard for Modifier 25 defensibility rests on four criteria:

1. Distinct Chief Complaint. The chief complaint must name a problem
   distinct from the procedure's primary indication. A chief complaint
   that reads as the procedure indication is a categorical failure.

2. Separate Exam Findings. The exam must document findings on a body
   part or condition not addressed by the procedure. Generic statements
   are insufficient.

3. Independent Medical Decision Making. The medical decision making
   must address a problem with risks, data, and management options
   independent of the procedure decision.

4. Site-Specificity. When the E/M and procedure are at different
   anatomic sites, both LT/RT or T1 to T9 modifiers must appear on the
   claim and the documentation must support the site distinction.

Each of these criteria stands on its own. A note that fails any one of
them does not support Modifier 25 under audit. Practices using Modifier
25 on more than 50 percent of procedural claims face Pre-Payment Reviews
in 2026 and beyond.
